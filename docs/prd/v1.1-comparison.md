## Problem Statement

Affordo answers "can I afford this?" one goal at a time, and it answers as though
that goal were the only one. Every saved Goal is measured against the whole of
the user's savings and the whole of their Monthly Disposable.

That is wrong in two ways, and the second is worse than the project has so far
recorded:

- **The flow is double-counted.** Three goals may each report "6 months" though
  they cannot all be funded at once. ADR 0007 point 3 calls this deliberate and
  names the fix as future work.
- **The stock is double-counted too, and this one is not optimism but falsehood.**
  A goal is judged affordable when current savings cover its price, and that test
  runs per goal against the entire balance. With €5,000 saved and two €5,000
  goals, Affordo reports the verdict **Afford on both**. Buying either one empties
  the pot the other's verdict depends on.

So a user with several goals is given a set of answers that cannot all be true,
with nothing on screen to say so. The question they actually have — _if I want
the laptop, what does it cost the holiday?_ — has no answer anywhere in the app.

## Solution

A goal may be given a **Share**: a monthly amount out of the Monthly Disposable
that the user assigns to it. A new screen, the **Comparison**, shows every saved
Goal, its Share, how long it takes at that Share, and its **Delay** — the months
it takes beyond what it would take alone. That Delay is the number behind
"buying X delays Y".

Shares are opt-in. A goal with no Share is **Unassigned**: it is not a slow goal,
it is a goal outside the Comparison — it draws nothing, releases nothing, and
shifts nobody else's Delay. A user who never visits the Comparison sees Affordo
behave exactly as it does today.

The Comparison is honest about the two things users actually do. If the Shares
total more than the disposable, the plan is **Overdrawn**: the screen says so and
still shows the timeline that plan implies, because Affordo has never blocked bad
news — only nonsense. And when a goal is funded, its Share reflows to the goals
still saving, so a date is never inflated by money that has already been freed.

Crucially, a goal's own verdict does not change. The Goals dashboard keeps
showing what the reference engine computes, unchanged. A Shared goal gains one
muted line carrying its Delay and linking to the Comparison.

## User Stories

1. As someone saving for several things at once, I want to assign each goal a monthly Share, so that I can decide how my surplus is divided instead of pretending each goal gets all of it.
2. As someone weighing two purchases, I want to see how many months each goal takes at its Share, so that I know when I would actually have each one.
3. As someone weighing two purchases, I want to see how much longer a goal takes because of the others, so that the cost of wanting both is a number rather than a feeling.
4. As a user who has not thought about splitting anything, I want Affordo to behave exactly as it does today, so that a new feature does not change answers I never asked it to change.
5. As a user, I want a goal with no Share assigned to be shown as outside the plan rather than as taking forever, so that not having decided yet is not reported as bad news.
6. As a user, I want to clear a goal's Share, so that I can take a goal out of the plan without deleting it.
7. As a user, I want to see the total I have assigned against what I actually have each month, so that I know whether my plan is real.
8. As a user who has assigned more than I earn, I want to be told plainly that the plan needs money I do not have, so that I am not misled by dates built on it.
9. As a user who has assigned more than I earn, I want to still see the resulting dates, so that I can see how far off the plan is and decide what to give up.
10. As a user, I want money freed by a completed goal to speed up my remaining goals, so that my dates reflect what will really happen rather than assuming I keep saving into something I already own.
11. As a user with money already saved, I want my existing savings counted toward my goals in the Comparison, so that the timeline does not start me from zero when it should not.
12. As a user whose savings already cover a goal, I want that goal shown as funded straight away, so that the plan starts from what is true today.
13. As a user whose savings overshoot a small goal, I want the excess to go to my other goals, so that no money is stranded against something already paid for.
14. As a user, I want to reach the Comparison from the main navigation, so that I can find it without knowing it exists.
15. As a user looking at a goal on my dashboard, I want to see that it is sharing with other goals and by how much it is delayed, so that I am not left believing a figure that assumes it is the only one.
16. As a user, I want that line on the goal card to take me straight to the Comparison, so that I can act on it where I noticed it.
17. As a user, I want the Comparison to use my chosen currency and number formatting, so that it reads like the rest of the app.
18. As a user, I want the Comparison to respect my light or dark theme, so that it does not look like a different application.
19. As a returning user, I want my Shares remembered, so that I do not rebuild my plan every visit.
20. As a user upgrading from a previous version, I want my saved goals to survive, so that adding a feature never costs me my data.
21. As a user upgrading from a previous version, I want my existing goals to start Unassigned, so that nothing is assumed on my behalf about how I would split my money.
22. As a user, I want editing my salary or expenses to update the whole Comparison at once, so that the plan reflects my current finances rather than a stale snapshot.
23. As a user, I want changing a goal's price to update its place in the Comparison, so that the plan stays true to what I am actually buying.
24. As a user, I want deleting a goal to remove it from the Comparison and free its Share, so that I am not planning around something I abandoned.
25. As a user whose expenses meet or exceed my income, I want the Comparison to tell me there is no surplus to divide, so that I get an honest answer rather than an impossible plan.
26. As a user with no surplus but some savings, I want to still see which goals my savings already cover, so that the screen does not withhold something true and useful.
27. As a user with no goals saved, I want the Comparison to tell me what it is for, so that an empty screen is not a dead end.
28. As a user with goals but none assigned, I want to be told how to start, so that I know the screen is waiting on me rather than broken.
29. As a user without a profile, I want to be sent to onboarding rather than shown an empty Comparison, so that the app never asks me to plan money it knows nothing about.
30. As a user on a touch device, I want every control on the Comparison — including the delay link on a goal card — to be comfortably tappable, so that I am not fighting the interface.
31. As a keyboard user, I want to reach and operate every Share input and control, so that the screen is usable without a mouse.
32. As a privacy-conscious user, I want my Shares to stay in my browser like everything else, so that adding a planning feature does not change where my financial data lives.
33. As a user reading the Comparison, I want it to look and read like the rest of Affordo, so that it does not feel like something bolted on.
34. As a user, I want the app's voice on this screen to challenge the plan and not me, so that being over-committed is presented as a choice to revisit rather than a failing.

## Implementation Decisions

The decisions below are recorded in full, with their rejected alternatives, in
**ADR 0024 (cross-goal contention: opt-in Shares on a separate route)** and
**ADR 0023 (net-new surface is governed by reference idiom, not reference
pixels)**. Vocabulary is in `CONTEXT.md` under _Competing goals_.

**Governance (ADR 0023).** The reference design this app reproduces has no
cross-goal feature, so the fidelity bar cannot be satisfied by measurement here.
The bar continues to govern every surface the reference has, unchanged. New
surface may only compose primitives already extracted from the reference — the
button and input base layers, the token palette, the card and dialog patterns,
the existing type scale. New composition of proven parts is permitted; new parts
are not. Where the reference gives no guidance on placement or emphasis, the
closest existing analogue governs, as the theme toggle took the Settings link's
treatment rather than inventing a header style. The review question is "does
every element trace to a snapshot primitive?"

**One new seam.** The engine module gains a single pure export beside the
existing verdict engine. All contention arithmetic lives behind it: the reflow
solve, the savings derivation, Delay, Overdrawn, the no-surplus rule, and
Unassigned handling. Above it there is nothing to compute, only rendering. The
engine stays framework-agnostic per ADR 0008 — the solve must not move into
React state, so that a future Android app can reuse it untouched.

The seam's contract, given as a type shape because it fixes several decisions
more precisely than prose:

```ts
compare(profile: ReferenceProfile, goals: Goal[]): Comparison;

interface Comparison {
  monthlyDisposable: number; // may be <= 0
  assigned: number; // sum of Shares
  overdrawn: boolean; // assigned > monthlyDisposable
  rows: ComparisonRow[]; // one per saved Goal, Shared or not
}

interface ComparisonRow {
  goalId: string;
  share: number | null; // null = Unassigned
  openingBalance: number; // derived share of savings
  months: number | null; // null when Unassigned or unreachable
  monthsAlone: number | null; // the solo baseline
  delay: number | null; // months - monthsAlone; null when either is
}
```

**The existing verdict engine is not touched.** Its signature, its arithmetic and
its output stay byte-identical. This is a hard constraint, not a preference — see
the baseline decision below.

**Shares divide the flow; savings follow the Share.** A Share is a monthly amount
out of the Monthly Disposable. A goal's opening balance is its share of existing
savings, derived from its share of the assigned monthly — there is no second
input and no second over-allocation state to validate. A goal whose opening
balance exceeds its price completes immediately and returns the surplus to the
solve.

**Reflow is proportional and event-driven.** When a goal is funded, its Share
redistributes among goals still saving, in proportion to their Shares. The solve
is therefore a sequence of events — find the earliest completion, advance to it,
redistribute, repeat — not a division. Without this, a €1,200 goal at €100/mo
reports 12 months when the true answer is 6, and it errs toward discouragement,
which ADR 0010 asks us not to do.

**Overdrawn is computed, never blocked.** Shares may total more than the
disposable. The Comparison reports the overdraw and still shows the implied
timeline. This follows how the app already treats a goal it cannot reach: it
computes it and names it, rather than preventing it. The input layer blocks
values that make the math nonsense (per ADR 0019); an over-committed plan is
arithmetically fine and merely untrue, which is a different thing.

**Unassigned is a state, not a rate.** A goal with no Share draws nothing,
releases nothing, and changes no other goal's Delay — it is genuinely inert.
Modelling it as an infinite duration would also strip it of its share of savings,
so a goal readable as Afford one moment would read as never the next. It also
removes a zero-denominator case: a Comparison with nothing assigned has nothing
to compute rather than a NaN to render.

**Delay computes its own solo baseline.** The existing verdict engine reports
months-to-save only on the stretch path; for any goal beyond the twelve-month
horizon it reports null and the dashboard falls back to rendering the cut horizon.
The baseline Delay needs therefore does not exist in the verdict output. The new
engine recomputes it with the same arithmetic, unconditionally. **Making the
verdict engine populate months-to-save unconditionally is a fidelity regression,
not a cleanup** — the goal card tests that field before falling back, so
populating it would silently change what every cut-to-afford card displays.

**No Verdict on the Comparison.** The four-way Verdict does not survive being
computed against a Share. Cut-to-afford means "reachable in twelve months if you
cut expenses", but cutting expenses raises the disposable for the whole plan,
which must then be re-shared before anyone knows who benefits — the lever is a
property of the Profile, not of a Goal. Three of the four kinds are coherent per
Share and one is not, so the Comparison shows months and Delay only. If extending
the Verdict to Shares is proposed later, refuse it.

**No surplus.** When the Monthly Disposable is zero or negative, Shares still
divide the savings pot, so a goal covered by its opening balance still reads as
funded. Everything else is unreachable, with no month figure and no Delay —
there is no delay when nothing was ever arriving. The screen says there is no
monthly surplus to share.

**The dashboard keeps its solo figures.** Every goal card renders exactly what
the verdict engine computes today. A Shared goal gains one muted line carrying
its Delay and linking to the Comparison. The dashboard calls the new engine once
and hands each card its row; **no component derives a Delay of its own.**

**The Comparison is the only place a Share is edited.** The goal dialog keeps its
three reference-verbatim fields. A Share is meaningless on a first goal — with
one goal there is nothing to compete for — and setting one without the other
goals and the running total in view is setting it blind.

**Persistence is additive at the current schema version.** The stored Goal gains
an optional Share. The schema version is deliberately **not** bumped: the store
discards records on a version mismatch rather than migrating them, so bumping it
would destroy every saved goal for every existing user. Absent already means
Unassigned, which is the correct reading of every goal saved before this feature,
so the migration is a no-op by construction. Per ADR 0019 a stored Share is
range-checked on load (finite, non-negative), not merely type-checked; the load
path's existing tolerance of unknown fields means old and new bundles interoperate
in both directions.

**Routing and navigation.** The Comparison gets its own path in the hand-rolled
router (ADR 0018), profile-guarded like the dashboard and settings. Its
navigation entry clones the existing Settings link's treatment rather than
inventing one, per ADR 0023 and the precedent set for the theme toggle in ADR 0021.

**Presentation.** Money and number formatting go through the existing locale
formatting used everywhere else, so the currency picker governs this screen too.
Months stay fractional and are not rounded up — ADR 0012's round-up was
superseded by ADR 0017's floating-point currency units, and the existing engine
does no rounding in its math.

**Hit targets.** Every interactive element on the Comparison, and the new delay
link on the goal card, meets 24×24 regardless of what the reference does. ADR
0022 exempts accessibility semantics from the fidelity bar but holds interactive
targets to that floor unconditionally. On the goal card this means growing the
target without changing the layout, the technique the header links already use.

## Testing Decisions

**What makes a good test here.** Assert external, observable behaviour: for the
engine, the Comparison returned for given inputs; for the UI, what a user sees
and can do. Never assert internal structure. Three project rules bind especially
hard on this feature:

- **The unit suite is blind to anything visual.** jsdom applies no stylesheet, so
  geometry, colour, focus rings and hit targets are unverified by it. Anything
  visual is pinned by a browser measurement or it is not pinned.
- **Assert one class per negative class assertion.** A negative assertion listing
  several classes passes when any one is absent, which has produced assertions
  that tested nothing.
- **Mutation-verify, and check the mutation is real.** Break the behaviour in the
  source, confirm the suite goes red. A test that re-derives the logic it is
  testing will stay green — which is the specific risk in this feature, since
  Delay is arithmetic that a test could easily reimplement in its own body.
  Execute the real engine.

**Engine (Vitest), near-exhaustive.** The primary seam and where wrong-money
advice would originate, so coverage is heaviest here. Table-driven against the
new export, mirroring the existing verdict engine's test as prior art. Cases:
every goal Unassigned; a mix of Shared and Unassigned; exact, under and over
allocation; the reflow sequence with two and three goals, including a goal funded
purely from its opening balance at month zero; an opening balance exceeding its
price and the surplus reflowing; Delay against a solo baseline that the verdict
engine reports as null; zero and negative disposable; zero savings; a single
Shared goal, where Delay must be zero; and the zero-denominator case where
nothing is assigned. Assert no output is NaN or negative in any case.

**Store (Vitest).** Extends the existing goals-store tests as prior art: a record
with no Share loads as Unassigned; a Share out of domain range is rejected on
load per ADR 0019; a Share round-trips through save and load; a record written by
a newer bundle still loads.

**Components (React Testing Library), thin.** The Comparison screen's states —
populated, all Unassigned, no goals, no surplus, Overdrawn — and the goal card's
delay line appearing only for a Shared goal and carrying the engine's number.
Class assertions only under the documented narrow exception, where colour or
geometry _is_ the requirement and the class is the only observable, with a
comment saying so.

**E2E (Playwright).** Everything the unit suite cannot see. The delay link and
every Comparison control measured against the 24×24 floor, extending the existing
target-size spec as prior art. Contrast on the Overdrawn warning in both themes.
The Comparison rendering correctly in light and dark. Privacy: no network request
carries a Share, extending the existing privacy spec.

**Features (Cucumber).** The journeys a stakeholder could confirm, in their
language — not pixels, class names or storage shapes. The existing goals feature
is the prior art for voice. Scenarios: assigning a Share to two goals and seeing
one delayed by the other; a goal left Unassigned staying out of the plan;
assigning more than I earn and being told so; clearing a Share; savings already
covering a goal; my plan surviving a reload. Phrase these as the settings feature
does — _"Affordo tells me the plan needs money I do not have"_, never _"the
overdrawn flag is true"_.

**Before opening a PR**, all five commands in the working agreements pass:
typecheck, unit, e2e, BDD, and build.

## Out of Scope

- **Fixing the dashboard's own double-count.** A goal's verdict stays the
  alone-figure. Two €5,000 goals against €5,000 saved will still both read Afford
  on their cards; only the delay line and the Comparison hint otherwise. This is
  a deliberate, recorded consequence of keeping the reference screen
  reference-faithful — not an oversight to be repaired.
- **Priority or ordering.** Goals are not ranked and there is no sequential
  waterfall. Contention is expressed by dividing the monthly, not by queueing.
- **Verdict badges on the Comparison**, and any extension of the four-way Verdict
  to a per-Share basis.
- **Editing a Share anywhere but the Comparison**, including the goal dialog.
- **Explicitly assigning savings** as a second per-goal number.
- **Interest, growth or compounding** in the reflow solve — ADR 0007 point 2
  stands.
- **Windfalls**, one-off lumps toward a goal. Never built; still not.
- **Camera Price Scan / OCR.** The v1.1 work of ADR 0005 is deferred
  indefinitely, pending an Android app that may not happen. This ships as v1.1 in
  its place.
- **Any backend, account or cross-device sync** — ADR 0004, ADR 0011.
- **Currency conversion or FX.** The picker stays cosmetic.
- **Schema migration machinery.** Deliberately not built, because this change
  does not need it and building it now would risk the data it exists to protect.

## Further Notes

- **Version numbering.** This is the cross-goal interaction ADR 0007 named as
  v1.2 work, shipping as **v1.1** because the original v1.1 (camera Price Scan,
  ADR 0005) is deferred indefinitely.
- **ADR 0007 is amended, not superseded.** Its instruction — _do not fix
  independent per-goal dates; word results so the optimism is visible_ — now
  binds the dashboard specifically rather than the app. Its point 3 also
  understated the problem: savings are double-counted as well as surplus.
- **Two words were unavailable and the collisions are load-bearing.**
  _Allocation_ is the banned synonym for Contribution and means the opposite here
  — a Contribution adds to the pot, a Share divides it. _Plan_ is the banned
  synonym for Goal. Both are recorded in the glossary's avoid lists.
- **The reference repository no longer exists.** It is archived in full in the
  repo. Any instruction in the history to fetch it live is dead. For this feature
  the archive will answer questions about primitives and tokens, and nothing
  about the feature itself, which the reference never had.
- **The weakest part of this design, for a reviewer to attack:** the proportional
  savings rule. It is derived rather than entered, which is what keeps the input
  burden at one number per goal, but it means a user who mentally earmarks a
  specific sum for a specific goal cannot say so, and the split they get may not
  be the one they intended. If that proves wrong in use, explicitly assigned
  savings is the recorded alternative.
